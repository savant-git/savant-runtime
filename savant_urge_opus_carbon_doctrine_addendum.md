# savant / urge–opus–carbon creative evolution

status: proposed doctrine addition
authority: pending acceptance
scope: creative iteration, provider execution, counterfactual exploration
implementation status: unverified

## 1. purpose

savant shall support creative evolution in which a user can
generate, refine, compare, branch, approve, and export visual
artifacts without surrendering control of approved design identity.

the initial application is logo evolution.

the implementation must remain reusable for other creative
media where existing savant capabilities support them.

## 2. ownership

urge owns actual iteration, improvement pressure, stopping
criteria, and the progression of creative revisions.

opus owns external model capability discovery, provider
selection, execution, normalization, and eligible fallback.

carbon owns counterfactual exploration and projections of
possible future creative trajectories.

existing authoritative state owners retain identity,
composition, lineage, provenance, history, and replay.

the interface presents and requests operations. it does
not establish competing authority or execution ownership.

## 3. iteration modes

two modes shall be exposed.

### continuity

continuity is enabled by default.

each accepted iteration references the preceding approved
baseline and the changes requested against that baseline.

the baseline includes the source artifact and its approved
constraints, not merely a textual description.

rejected candidates do not automatically replace the baseline.

approved elements may be locked against unintended change.

exact preservation must use deterministic editing when
generative editing cannot guarantee the required fidelity.

### exploration

exploration explicitly relaxes continuity requirements.

the user may permit changes to composition, geometry,
typography, style, material, and other eligible properties.

exploration retains candidate lineage and provenance.

switching modes does not destroy earlier approved states.

## 4. creative state

a creative session references:

- its original brief;
- approved design constraints;
- current approved baseline;
- candidate artifacts;
- requested transformations;
- provider execution records;
- evaluations and their evidence;
- user decisions;
- immutable revision relationships.

authoritative substance is stored once.

secondary views are deterministically projected.

provider-generated artifacts remain distinct from
user-approved canonical artifacts.

## 5. image generation and editing

opus selects eligible external image capabilities according
to the requested operation.

eligible operations may include:

- initial image generation;
- reference-conditioned generation;
- targeted image editing;
- visual evaluation;
- segmentation or masking;
- image transformation;
- image enhancement.

capability availability must be established through actual
provider contracts and execution evidence.

providers shall not be assumed interchangeable.

provider-specific parameters are normalized at the opus
boundary without becoming canonical savant semantics.

secrets must remain isolated from generated artifacts,
browser responses, logs, and user-facing exports.

## 6. deterministic design construction

where exact geometry is required, the system should prefer
deterministic vector or raster operations.

canonical vector geometry and cinematic presentation
renderings are distinct artifacts.

a presentation rendering must not silently redefine the
canonical geometry.

vector exports must contain genuine vector geometry.
raster images embedded in vector containers do not qualify
as reconstructed editable vector designs.

## 7. evaluation

urge may request evaluations through opus.

evaluations may consider:

- adherence to the brief;
- visual distinctiveness;
- geometric coherence;
- baseline preservation;
- typography and legibility;
- small-scale reproduction;
- monochrome suitability;
- consistency across variants;
- production usability;
- user preferences.

evaluation outputs are evidence or estimates, not
independent artistic authority.

a score must identify its evaluator, criteria, inputs,
and limitations.

user approval remains authoritative for subjective
creative choices unless higher authority states otherwise.

## 8. carbon counterfactual exploration

carbon may derive alternative hypothetical trajectories
from an identified creative state.

each trajectory records:

- its parent state reference;
- parameter changes;
- creative persona or evaluation perspective;
- iteration horizon;
- exploration budget;
- simulation method;
- estimated outcomes;
- uncertainty and evidence;
- dependencies on actual provider execution.

counterfactual states are explicitly non-authoritative.

they must not mutate the approved urge session.

superposition denotes maintained alternative hypothetical
possibilities unless an actual quantum-computing backend
and corresponding evidence justify a stronger claim.

carbon must not claim knowledge of future generated images
that have not been generated or adequately evaluated.

## 9. adaptive lookahead

carbon may apply bounded search across hypothetical
trajectories.

the search may use:

- branch expansion;
- candidate pruning;
- diversity preservation;
- uncertainty-aware selection;
- progressive allocation of compute;
- multi-objective evaluation;
- selective deeper execution;
- comparison against observed outcomes.

a twenty-five-round horizon is a configurable planning
target, not a guarantee of twenty-five accurate predictions.

estimated outcomes must remain distinguishable from
executed outcomes.

the system should stop exploring when additional expected
value does not justify additional cost or risk.

## 10. future-aware selection

carbon may present alternative recommendations including:

- conservative continuity;
- strongest estimated final outcome;
- exploratory high-variance outcome.

recommendations must expose material tradeoffs.

urge may consume the selected recommendation as an
iteration proposal.

only an authorized execution can advance the real session.

## 11. prediction calibration

when a hypothetical trajectory is subsequently executed,
its observed outcome may be compared with its prediction.

calibration records must preserve the original prediction.

prediction errors must not be rewritten as successes.

repeated poor calibration shall reduce confidence in the
corresponding lookahead method.

## 12. resilience

provider failure must not destroy approved artifacts.

retries must not silently duplicate accepted revisions.

interrupted operations must retain recoverable references
to completed work.

partial candidate output must not be represented as an
accepted completed design.

resource budgets must bound iteration count, provider
calls, concurrent execution, and counterfactual search.

## 13. user interface

the initial interface shall provide:

- creative brief entry;
- generation controls;
- candidate comparison;
- approval and rejection;
- default-on baseline continuity;
- unrestricted exploration mode;
- targeted refinement instructions;
- approved-element preservation;
- visible iteration lineage;
- counterfactual branch comparison when available;
- execution status and meaningful failures;
- final artifact export.

counterfactual results must be visually distinguishable
from real generated artifacts.

the interface shall support keyboard navigation, visible
focus, reduced motion, and readable contrast.

## 14. integration boundaries

no new exile is required solely to provide a visual page.

no new provider registry may duplicate opus.

no new iteration controller may duplicate urge.

no new counterfactual authority may duplicate carbon.

no independent history store may compete with existing
authoritative history ownership.

additional components require demonstrated dependency
and compatibility with current accepted architecture.

## 15. minimum executable proof

the first functional proof shall demonstrate:

1. a user submits a creative brief;
2. opus executes a real eligible image provider;
3. urge receives a generated candidate;
4. the user approves that candidate;
5. the user requests a targeted refinement;
6. the approved candidate is passed as the actual baseline;
7. opus executes the required editing capability;
8. urge evaluates and presents the new candidate;
9. both versions remain accessible;
10. the user can export the selected result.

the second proof may introduce carbon branching.

carbon must not be presented as operational until a real
branching execution and corresponding evidence exist.

## 16. acceptance conditions

the implementation is acceptable only when:

- existing authority and contracts are preserved;
- actual execution is distinguished from simulation;
- approved baseline continuity is demonstrated;
- user rejection does not advance the baseline;
- lineage and provenance remain available;
- provider failures are recoverable;
- no unverified capability is advertised as working;
- required focused verification succeeds.

## 17. implementation boundary

this document specifies proposed behavior.

it does not assert that urge, opus, or carbon currently
implement these capabilities.

existing implementations must be inspected before their
contracts are extended.

acceptance into the master doctrine requires reconciliation
against current authoritative sources and decisions.
