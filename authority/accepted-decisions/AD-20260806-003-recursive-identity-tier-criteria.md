## Decision

Savant adopts the recursive identity-tier criteria defined by:

`/root/savant-runtime/canon/structure/SAVANT_IDENTITY_TIER_CRITERIA_v1.0.0.json`

The accepted identity hierarchy is:

`iota → mote → trait → quirk → prodigal → exile → innate → portal → obelisk`

The accepted emergent progression is:

`Identity → Coherence → Disposition → Behavior → Capability → Agency → Faculty → Access → Sovereignty`

Every substantiated identity tier above Iota must be composed from at least two distinct, active, qualifying instances of the tier immediately beneath it.

Canonical lower-tier substance must remain stored once. Higher tiers compose lower tiers through stable identity references, typed composition edges, contracts, lineage, provenance, dependencies, compatibility declarations, and validation receipts.

Physical path, filename, directory depth, file count, implementation size, age, popularity, anticipated reuse, or historical placement do not establish identity tier, ownership, or authority.

An element qualifies for a tier only when it satisfies:

1. the universal instance criteria;
2. the tier-specific constitutive criteria;
3. adjacent-tier composition requirements;
4. the tier-specific emergent property;
5. substantiation proof;
6. authority, lineage, and provenance requirements;
7. dependency and compatibility requirements;
8. non-duplication requirements;
9. applicable validation and receipt requirements.

An element that lacks qualifying plural constituents or cannot prove its tier-specific emergence remains one of:

- template;
- proposal;
- incomplete candidate;
- compatibility surface;
- migration staging object;
- unresolved artifact.

It must not claim substantiated identity-tier status.

## Universal composition law

For every identity instance `x` at rank `r > 1`:

1. every direct constituent of `x` must be a valid active instance of rank `r - 1`;
2. the number of distinct direct constituents must be at least two;
3. every constituent must be referenced by stable identity;
4. constituent canonical substance must not be copied into the parent;
5. the composition must produce the parent rank’s distinct emergent property;
6. no individual constituent may independently supply that complete emergent property;
7. composition must be deterministic or explicitly declare governed nondeterminism;
8. authority, lineage, provenance, dependency, compatibility, and evidence must remain traversable;
9. failure to prove any requirement prevents substantiation.

## Tier meanings

### Iota

An Iota is the smallest identity-bearing canonical unit whose division changes meaning or authority.

Its emergence is Identity.

### Mote

A Mote composes Iotas into one coherent micro-capability.

Its emergence is Coherence.

### Trait

A Trait composes Motes into one stable observable property.

Its emergence is Disposition.

### Quirk

A Quirk composes Traits into one bounded conditional behavioral pattern.

Its emergence is Behavior.

### Prodigal

A Prodigal composes Quirks into one autonomous reusable capability governed by a stable contract.

Its emergence is Capability.

### Exile

An Exile composes Prodigals into one domain-responsible semantic owner with an enduring operational jurisdiction.

Its emergence is Agency.

### Innate

An Innate composes Exiles into one constitutional faculty intrinsic to its containing Portal.

Its emergence is Faculty.

### Portal

A Portal composes Innates into one governed interaction boundary through which capabilities are admitted, exposed, translated, or traversed.

Its emergence is Access.

Portal remains the eighth identity level. Historical identity paths using `gate` are compatibility evidence and must not be destructively renamed without a separately authorized migration. Programming gates remain gates.

### Obelisk

An Obelisk composes Portals into one sovereign foundational Savant system with a complete durable purpose.

Its emergence is Sovereignty.

## Moods

The accepted criterion pipeline uses:

`anima → weld → kiln → graft → aria → mantle → fulcrum → echelon → ascent`

Moods are modular operators. They are not identity tiers, semantic owners, or mandatory physical directories.

Their accepted functions are:

- Anima establishes genuine instance identity.
- Weld composes distinct constituents without erasing identity.
- Kiln hardens composition through validation and replay.
- Graft attaches optional extensions without replacement or duplication.
- Aria produces projections without creating authority.
- Mantle applies jurisdiction, authority, custody, policy, and security.
- Fulcrum evaluates balance, cohesion, coupling, cardinality, capacity, and dependency direction.
- Echelon proves legitimate emergence into the next tier.
- Ascent compiles validated composition toward higher-order operation.

## Recursive identity scaffold

The hierarchy forms a recursive identity scaffold.

- Stable instances are scaffold nodes.
- Adjacent-tier composition relations are vertical members.
- Kindred, authority, dependency, provenance, compatibility, and Cabal relations are horizontal members.
- Segues are typed roads between adjacent tiers.
- Typed transit envelopes travel through Segues.

A typed transit envelope preserves:

- identity;
- source;
- destination;
- payload reference;
- contract;
- authority witness;
- lineage;
- provenance;
- dependency context;
- compatibility context;
- mood bindings;
- slot bindings;
- validity;
- expiration;
- receipt policy.

Transit must not duplicate canonical substance or erase source identity.

## Runtime shards

Runtime shardization is established as a future deterministic projection mechanism.

The following are accepted:

1. runtime shards are not the second identity tier;
2. Mote remains the second identity tier;
3. canonical instances remain authoritative;
4. shards do not replace canonical instances;
5. shards must derive deterministically from canonical identity;
6. shards must retain source identity, digest, lineage, provenance, authority witness, and replay information.

The following remain unresolved:

- shard format;
- shard granularity;
- shard boundaries;
- shard lifecycle;
- routing;
- distribution;
- recombination;
- persistence;
- expiration;
- failure handling;
- authority refresh;
- cross-Portal traversal.

No implementation may invent or hardcode unresolved shard semantics without a later accepted decision.
