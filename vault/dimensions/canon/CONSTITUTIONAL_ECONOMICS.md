# CONSTITUTIONAL ECONOMICS
Version: 1.2.0
Status: Canonical

---

# Purpose

Constitutional Economics governs the efficient use of authority, complexity, storage, computation, and maintenance.

Every architectural decision carries cost.

The constitution seeks the lowest long-term total cost while preserving correctness.

---

# Principles

Architectural economics shall be:

- measurable
- deterministic
- evidence-based
- replayable
- observable
- composable
- sustainable
- minimal
- constitutional

---

# Nine Economic Laws

1. Authority Economy
2. Complexity Economy
3. Storage Economy
4. Computational Economy
5. Maintenance Economy
6. Evolution Economy
7. Recovery Economy
8. Operational Economy
9. Human Economy

---

# Authority Economy

Prefer:

- one authority
- zero duplication
- explicit ownership
- deterministic projection
- governed admission
- immutable history
- minimal truth
- reusable primitives
- constitutional simplicity

---

# Complexity Economy

Reduce:

- unnecessary abstraction
- unnecessary indirection
- duplicated logic
- competing representations
- manual synchronization
- special cases
- hidden coupling
- architectural debt
- cognitive load

Complexity must purchase measurable capability.

---

# Storage Economy

Persist only:

- authority
- evidence
- lineage
- provenance
- immutable history
- identities
- accepted decisions
- constitutional metadata
- recovery metadata

Everything else should be regenerated.

---

# Computational Economy

Optimize:

- replay cost
- graph traversal
- validation
- indexing
- regeneration
- projection
- dependency analysis
- integrity verification
- scheduling

Speed never overrides correctness.

---

# Maintenance Economy

Reduce future work through:

- normalization
- composition
- reusable instances
- typed segues
- validator reuse
- policy reuse
- deterministic generation
- automation
- constitutional governance

---

# Evolution Economy

Prefer designs that maximize:

- extensibility
- attachment
- compatibility
- migration safety
- future optionality
- replay safety
- validator evolution
- schema evolution
- subsystem independence

---

# Recovery Economy

Recovery shall require only:

- authoritative history
- accepted decisions
- evidence
- schemas
- validators
- constitutional canon
- deterministic replay
- manifests
- integrity verification

---

# Operational Economy

Continuously reduce:

- operational overhead
- manual intervention
- synchronization effort
- recovery time
- validation cost
- deployment risk
- maintenance risk
- failure impact
- observability gaps

---

# Human Economy

Architectures shall minimize:

- cognitive load
- manual bookkeeping
- repetitive maintenance
- ambiguity
- duplicated knowledge
- hidden assumptions
- documentation drift
- operator error
- unnecessary decisions

Humans govern.

The system performs repetition.

---

# Economic Evaluation

Every proposed mutation evaluates:

- authority cost
- implementation cost
- maintenance cost
- storage cost
- runtime cost
- replay cost
- recovery cost
- migration cost
- long-term ownership cost

---

# Constitutional Guarantee

The constitution optimizes for minimum lifetime cost while preserving authority, correctness, replayability, recoverability, and extensibility.
