# savant knowledge metabolism canon

## status

This canon governs selective learning, refresh, knowledge-gap
resolution, and active acquisition within the living epistemic fabric.

## purpose

Savant must not ingest information merely because information exists.

Knowledge metabolism determines, as a derived bounded decision process,
whether potential information is worth acquiring, admitting, refreshing,
challenging, activating, deferring, or ignoring.

Knowledge metabolism is not truth authority.

## value dimensions

A metabolism projection may consider:

-   task relevance;
-   novelty;
-   source authority;
-   expected uncertainty reduction;
-   expected contradiction resolution;
-   expected future reuse;
-   freshness;
-   dependency importance;
-   downstream impact;
-   source diversity;
-   acquisition cost;
-   latency;
-   security exposure;
-   privacy constraints;
-   and resource budget.

No single dimension automatically governs admission.

## knowledge gaps

A knowledge gap is a derived condition arising from an active task,
unresolved contradiction, insufficient evidence, stale dependency,
missing provenance, unresolved semantic identity, or unsatisfied
epistemic contract.

A gap does not prove that the missing information exists or is
obtainable.

## demand propagation

A Savant consumer may expose a bounded epistemic requirement.

That requirement may project into one or more knowledge gaps.

A gap may project into acquisition candidates.

Acquired material remains untrusted until it passes the normal Savant
normalization, provenance, verification, evidence-admission, and
authority boundaries.

## active acquisition

Active acquisition must be bounded by permissions, task relevance,
resource budgets, security boundaries, privacy boundaries, source
policies, expected information gain, and explicit termination
conditions.

Savant must not research indefinitely merely because uncertainty
remains.

## predictive maintenance

Refresh priority may increase when admitted dependencies establish that
information is likely to become stale or materially important.

Predictive maintenance must be driven by evidenced dependency and
freshness relationships rather than arbitrary topic guessing.

## freshness

Freshness policy is domain-sensitive.

Weather, software interfaces, legal rules, provider capabilities,
scientific results, mathematical theorems, historical events, and
accepted Savant decisions cannot share one universal aging rule.

Freshness remains subordinate to authority.

## epistemic homeostasis

Knowledge metabolism should balance:

-   learning and stability;
-   novelty and reliability;
-   exploration and cost;
-   freshness and historical continuity;
-   specificity and generalization;
-   confidence and uncertainty;
-   compression and provenance preservation.

Continuous learning must not become uncontrolled mutation.

## selective activation

Possessing knowledge does not require activating it for every task.

Only task-relevant knowledge should be projected into a consumer's
active context.

Dormant information remains historically recoverable.

Removing information from active projection is not deletion.

## incremental invalidation

When admitted knowledge materially changes, dependent derived
projections should be invalidated through explicit dependency
relationships.

Where practical, recomputation should be local.

Historical evidence is preserved.

## outcome learning

Verified outcomes may provide evidence about the performance of
acquisition, selection, reasoning, and composition strategies.

They do not retroactively make prior conclusions true.

Savant output cannot validate itself merely because Savant produced it.

## external technology

Retrieval frameworks, search engines, vector databases, embedding
systems, graph engines, external providers, libraries, and public
repositories are implementation resources only.

They may accelerate acquisition or projection but cannot become hidden
architectural authority.

Dependencies are justified only when their material value exceeds their
complexity, coupling, replay, security, maintenance, and supply-chain
costs.

## completion condition

Knowledge metabolism is complete only when acquisition and refresh are
bounded, gap-driven where appropriate, provenance-preserving,
authority-safe, resource-aware, replayable at the decision level, and
incapable of silently promoting retrieved or generated material into
truth.
