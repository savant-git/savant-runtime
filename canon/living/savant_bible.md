## status

planned integrated knowledge system / not yet accepted as independent authority

## intended runtime path

`/root/savant-runtime/canon/living/savant_bible.md`

## purpose of this file

This document defines the maximal production-grade concept for the future Savant Bible.

It does not, merely by existing, supersede:

- current user directives;
- the accepted authoritative graph;
- accepted decisions;
- constitutional canon;
- the Savant master doctrine;
- accepted specifications;

It defines what the Savant Bible is intended to become, the semantic boundaries it must preserve, the capabilities it may eventually support, the requirements for promotion into authority, and the architecture by which it can become comprehensive without becoming a duplicate source of truth.

The Bible is intended to be the most complete integrated human-readable expression of Savant as a whole.

It should eventually explain, connect, navigate, contextualize, and project the total Savant system across:

# 1. irreducible conception

The Savant Bible is the integrated explanatory body of Savant.

The master doctrine defines the constitutional kernel: the durable cross-domain rules that govern how Savant behaves.

The Bible surrounds that kernel with the full multidisciplinary body of knowledge required to understand and operate Savant expertly.

The Bible therefore has a different purpose from the master doctrine.

## master doctrine

The master doctrine answers:

- how should Savant fundamentally behave?
- what universal principles govern authority, truth, composition, execution, preservation, evolution, and quality?
- what behavior rules apply across domains?

## Savant Bible

The Bible answers:

- what is Savant?
- why does it exist?
- how did it evolve?
- what does each part mean?
- how do its systems relate?
- what expert knowledge does Savant use across domains?
- what capabilities exist?
- what capabilities could exist?
- what is active?
- what is latent?
- what is authoritative?
- what is implementation?
- what is historical?
- how should specialists operate within every supported domain?
- how should a future human or machine navigate the entire system without confusing explanation with ownership?

The Bible should be able to teach Savant to a new contributor, guide an expert performing sophisticated work, explain its architecture to a maintainer, orient a creative director, support a technical implementer, and provide a coherent navigational map over the entire system.

It should accomplish all of those roles without becoming a second database, a second authority graph, or a second specification registry.

# 2. authority position

The Bible must have an explicit and limited authority role.

The governing precedence remains:

1. current user directive;
2. accepted authoritative graph;
3. accepted decisions;
4. constitutional canon, including the accepted Savant master doctrine;
5. verified implementation;
6. admitted evidence;
7. deterministic projection;
8. historical implementation;
9. historical documents;
10. inference;
11. speculation.

The completed Bible may become an accepted canonical synthesis layer, but it must not silently move above this order.

The Bible may contain material drawn from multiple authority levels, but every such section must preserve the semantic role of its source.

For example:

- a doctrine chapter may summarize constitutional doctrine;
- a decision chapter may index accepted decisions;
- a specification chapter may explain exact accepted contracts;
- an implementation chapter may describe verified current behavior;
- a history chapter may preserve superseded implementation;
- a future-capability chapter may describe latent possibilities.

These sections must never collapse into one undifferentiated “truth” layer.

# 3. core architectural law

The Bible should follow Savant-native architecture:

**substantiate once → instance → compose → relate through typed segues → project**

Applied to the Bible:

## substantiate once

The owning authority remains the canonical source.

Examples:

- master doctrine owns constitutional behavior;
- accepted decisions own immutable choices;
- accepted specifications own exact accepted values;
- verified implementation owns current operational reality;
- evidence sources own admitted observations.

## instance

The Bible may create contextual references or instances of those sources for explanation.

An instance may include:

- display title;
- summary;
- local context;
- relationship description;
- navigation metadata;
- teaching notes.

It must not silently duplicate or mutate authoritative substance.

## compose

The Bible composes references into higher-order explanations.

For example, a chapter on Savant identity may compose:

- doctrine principles;
- accepted brand decisions;
- exact identity specification;
- verified implementation;
- historical evolution.

## typed segues

Relationships should be explicit when meaningful.

Examples:

- `governed_by`
- `supersedes`
- `implements`
- `projects_from`
- `depends_on`
- `illustrates`
- `contradicts`
- `derived_from`
- `historical_predecessor`
- `future_capability_of`

## project

The Bible may generate or support derived views such as:

- domain chapters;
- timelines;
- glossaries;
- capability matrices;
- architecture maps;
- decision histories;
- dependency maps;
- onboarding guides;
- specialist views.

Those projections must remain rebuildable from authoritative sources.

# 5. why one Bible is stronger than separate doctrine files

Separate doctrine files would create several risks:

- duplicated universal principles;
- inconsistent terminology;
- cross-file drift;
- conflicting updates;
- ambiguous authority;
- difficult navigation;
- manual synchronization;
- fragmented understanding;
- unnecessary routing complexity.

A single Bible can preserve modularity through chapters, stable anchors, generated indexes, and internal typed relationships without creating multiple doctrinal truth systems.

The Bible should therefore be:

**one authoritative integrated body**
with
**many internally modular knowledge domains**

rather than
**many separately authoritative doctrine documents**.

# 7. capability-state model

The Bible should use a precise capability vocabulary.

## conceivable

A capability is technically imaginable but has not been evaluated for Savant.

## admissible

A capability has been evaluated and is compatible with Savant architecture in principle.

## latent

A capability is intentionally preserved as a plausible future option but is not implemented.

## implemented

Verified implementation exists.

## available

The implementation is usable in the current environment.

## activated

The capability is participating in the current task or runtime flow.

## accepted

The capability is explicitly accepted as part of Savant's intended system.

## deprecated

The capability remains temporarily supported but is being replaced.

## retired

The capability is no longer active but its historical role may remain documented.

The Bible must never describe a latent or conceivable capability as implemented.

# 12. doctrine integration model

The Bible should absorb specialized doctrine as chapters.

It should not absorb the master doctrine by silently duplicating it.

Instead:

master doctrine
→ constitutional source

Bible
→ constitutional explanation + specialist expansion

A Bible domain chapter should begin from the master principles that apply and then deepen them.

Example:

master doctrine:
“derived information should be projected rather than stored as competing authority.”

brand chapter:
“responsive logo variants should derive from canonical identity geometry.”

software chapter:
“indexes and caches should derive from authoritative data.”

editorial chapter:
“alternate reading views should derive from canonical content structure.”

The Bible therefore allows one master principle to become many domain-specific expert practices without duplicating constitutional authority.

# 24. dynamic versus static content

The Bible should separate stable and volatile knowledge.

## stable

Examples:

- doctrine;
- terminology;
- architecture concepts;
- expert methods;
- accepted identity principles.

## moderately changing

Examples:

- accepted decisions;
- accepted specifications;
- capability state.

## volatile

Examples:

- current implementation status;
- current providers;
- live dependencies;
- current performance;
- active task state.

Volatile information should be referenced or projected rather than manually embedded deeply throughout the Bible.

This reduces drift.

# 25. versioning

Every accepted Bible edition should have a version.

Version change should correspond to semantic change, not every trivial formatting edit.

Possible version categories:

- major: architectural or semantic reorganization;
- minor: substantial new domain or capability;
- patch: clarification without changed governing meaning.

Do not enforce semantic versioning until accepted.

The principle is stable lineage.

---

# 26. immutable historical editions

Accepted Bible editions should be preserved.

Do not silently rewrite history.

If the Bible becomes generated from structured sources, generated editions should still be reproducible from source/version metadata.

Historical editions may be archived rather than kept in the living path.

# 27. Bible compilation

The long-term strongest implementation may treat the Bible as a compiled projection.

Potential model:

authoritative primitives
+ doctrine
+ decisions
+ specifications
+ verified implementation metadata
+ evidence references
→ deterministic Bible compiler
→ integrated human-readable Bible

This is a latent capability.

It should not be implemented until manual maintenance becomes a credible drift problem.

The compiler would be valuable because it could:

- eliminate duplicate manual updates;
- preserve source references;
- regenerate indexes;
- ensure consistent status labels;
- expose missing lineage;
- build domain projections;
- validate anchors;
- generate navigation.

This may ultimately be the correct mature architecture.

It is not required now.

# 46. scale

The Bible may eventually become extremely large.

Its architecture should therefore support:

- stable anchors;
- generated table of contents;
- domain indexes;
- cross references;
- partial projections;
- section-level retrieval;
- versioned compilation.

Do not split it into independent doctrine authorities merely because it becomes long.

Physical storage segmentation may be acceptable if a deterministic compiler presents one logical Bible.

Logical unity matters more than one-file purity.

---

# 47. physical segmentation versus logical unity

If file size eventually becomes operationally problematic, the Bible may be physically segmented into source modules while remaining one logical compiled artifact.

Example:

`bible/source/brand.md`
`bible/source/software.md`
`bible/source/security.md`

could compile into:

`savant_bible.md`

In that model, the physical files are source modules, not independent doctrines.

This is a possible mature implementation.

It should not be introduced until file size, collaboration, or tooling justifies it.

---

# 48. incremental rebuild

A future Bible compiler should ideally support incremental rebuild.

If one source changes, only dependent sections need recomputation.

Potential benefits:

- faster generation;
- localized validation;
- clearer lineage;
- reduced unnecessary diff noise.

This is an optimization for credible scale, not current necessity.

---

# 49. deterministic diff

Future Bible versions should be diffable.

Stable ordering and formatting help distinguish substantive changes from formatting noise.

This improves:

- review;
- lineage;
- governance;
- rollback.

---

# 50. editorial quality

The Bible should be technically exhaustive without becoming unreadable.

Editorial principles:

- strong edifice;
- precise terminology;
- no filler;
- no unnecessary repetition;
- concrete examples where they clarify;
- explicit status labels;
- concise summaries before deep sections;
- cross references instead of repeated substance.

Comprehensiveness must increase usable understanding.

Volume alone is not comprehensiveness.

Do not allow copied examples to harden into unreviewed specifications.

- higher source governs.

- do not overwrite the last accepted Bible with partial output.
